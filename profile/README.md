## Calculator Overview

G-Wizard tackles one of machining's trickiest questions: what feed and speed will cut cleanly without destroying the tool or the part. Rather than relying on rough handbook numbers, it factors in the specific material, cutter geometry, depth of cut, and even the rigidity limits of your machine. The result is a recommendation tuned to your actual setup instead of a generic starting point.

The calculator's strength lies in how many variables it juggles at once. Tool deflection, chip thinning, rigidity, and horsepower all influence the safe cutting window, and G-Wizard surfaces those trade-offs so you understand why a number is what it is. For newer machinists, this transforms guesswork into guided decisions; for veterans, it confirms instincts and flags overlooked limits.

Paired with the calculator is a G-code editor that helps read, analyze, and clean up programs. Together they form a planning companion that reduces broken tools, poor finishes, and wasted stock, letting shops of any size push their equipment to a sensible edge with far more confidence.

## Getting Started Playbook

Launch G-Wizard and enter the essentials of your job: the material you are cutting, the tool you plan to use, and the machine's capabilities. As you fill in these details, the calculator produces a recommended feed and speed along with related figures like chip load and spindle power. Starting with an accurate description of your setup is the key to trustworthy output.

Review the suggestions and any warnings, such as excessive deflection, then adjust depth of cut or stepover to bring the numbers into a comfortable range. Apply the values at your machine and observe the actual cut, refining your inputs as you learn how your equipment behaves. Over time, saved presets make repeat jobs even faster to plan.
